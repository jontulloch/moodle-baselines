# 09 Summative Links with Completion Criteria

It is important to ensure that links for submitting summative assessments are available for students as soon as possible. This is to ensure students can gain confidence in knowing precisely where those assessments are to be submitted. At the same time, associating ‘completion criteria’ with those assessments helps to make it clearly to students that it is a required activity - and it helps educators to more easily track submissions. This minimum standard is therefore really a combination of two things: The availability of summative assessment links, and the use of completion tracking.

Please note that this report is only relevant to modules where students are submitting their summative assessment via Moodle. By default, modules are created assuming that ‘Students are submitting via Moodle’. This is because Moodle submissions remain the most common form of summative submission. However, this can be changed in the course settings. In the course settings, scroll down to the section titled ‘Moodle Minimum Standards’, and select the appropriate submission option from the drop-down list:


![](/assets/gitbook/image-77.png)


When this setting is changed from Moodle, the minimum standard **09 Are summative assessment links available, with completion criteria?** automatically changes to ‘not applicable’. It will show up as grey on the progress bar, and as ‘n/a’ in the summary report. This detailed report will therefore not be needed.

### Appearance

The top of the detailed report table explains the criteria through which the pass / fail result has been achieved on the summary report and the in the progress bar. Below that explanation, there is a list of all assignments, quizzes, Turnitin activities and any other activities tagged as being ‘summative’:


![](/assets/gitbook/image-78.png)


The report is organised into 4 columns. The first column shows the name of the assessment activity - together with an associated icon for easy identification. The second column shows a simple ‘tick’ if the activity is in the ‘Assessment & Feedback’ section of the Moodle page. The third section identifies if the activity has any completion criteria associated with it, and whether those criteria are manual (i.e. a student has to ‘tick’ to complete the activity) or automatic (the student has to complete an action, like submitting an assignment, after which the activity automatically records as complete). The fourth column shows whether a ‘summative’ or ‘formative’ tag has been added to the activity.

For the minimum standards conditions to me met, there has to be at least 1 assessment activity in the Assessment & Feedback section (green tick in the second column), AND with completion criteria added to it (indicated in the third column). Activities meeting these criteria are listed at the top of the report. Assessment activities that do not meet both these criteria are listed at the bottom of the report and are greyed out.

### What it does

The report scans the course page to identify any activity that might be considered an ‘assessment’ activity. The main activities in Moodle used for assessments are:

* Moodle Assignment
* Turnitin
* Quiz

However, there are a huge range of options that could be used for assessments. This report focuses on these three in order that the list of matching activities is not overlong. To ensure that other activities can be included in this list where used, all activities in Moodle can be assigned a ‘summative’ or a ‘formative’ tag via the activity settings. Any activity with a ‘summative’ or ‘formative’ tag is included in the report, regardless of the activity type.


![](/assets/gitbook/image-79.png)


Activities with a ‘summative’ tag will have a ‘summative’ badge showing in the fourth column. Activities with a ‘formative’ tag will have a ‘formative’ badge showing in the fourth column. Activities with a formative tag will be faintly grayed out, to indicate that these activities are not included in the criteria for the minimum standard.


![](/assets/gitbook/image-80.png)


The report then identifies which section the activity sits inside on the course page. All summative links are expected to appear in the Assessment & Feedback section. If the activity is in the Assessment & Feedback section, then a green tick will appear in the column ‘Assessment & Feedback Section’:


![](/assets/gitbook/image-81.png)


Finally the report checks the activities to see if they have completion criteria assigned to them. The outcome of this check will appear in the third column. There are three possible outcomes:



* Manual criteria: the student manually ticks the activity to say they have completed it.
* Automatic criteria: the activity is automatically marked as complete when certain criteria are met.
* None: there are no completion criteria assigned to the activity.


![](/assets/gitbook/image-82.png)


### How to use it

If your students are not making their summative assessments via Moodle, then you do not need to take any further action in relation to this detailed report. All you need to do is navigate to the course settings


![](/assets/gitbook/image-83.png)


Scroll down to the section titled ‘Moodle Minimum Standards’ and change the setting from ‘Students are submitting via Moodle’ to any relevant alternative. This Minimum Standard will show up as grey on the progress bar, and as ‘n/a’ in the summary report. This detailed report will therefore not be needed.

Otherwise, to meet the criteria for the minimum standard for summative assessment links with completion criteria, an activity needs to meet three conditions:

* ✔ Be in the Assessment & Feedback section.
* ✔ Have completion criteria added to it.
* ✔ Be a Turnitin, Assignment, Quiz activity, OR have a ‘summative’ tag.

The ‘Assessment & Feedback’ section is where the report looks for summative assessments. This is partly to promote increases consistency of student experience, and partly because it helps to make the report more accurate and reflective of your Moodle course page:


![](/assets/gitbook/image-84.png)


If your course does not have any Assignments, Turnitin activities, Quizzes, or other activities tagged as ‘summative’, then the detailed report will show a warning:

> **“This course has no Assignments, Quizzes, Turnitin activities, or tagged assessment activities. If students are not submitting via Moodle, please adjust the course settings to show this. Scroll down to the Minimum Standards section and choose the relevant option from the menu. Otherwise, you do need to include assessment submission links on your course page”**


![](/assets/gitbook/image-85.png)


If your course does have assessment activities, but none are in the Assessment & Feedback section, there will be no ticks in the column ‘Assessment & Feedback Section’ and you will see a warning saying:

> **“You do not seem to have any assessment activities in the Assessment & Feedback section. Summative assessments should be in this section in order to ensure a consistent experience for students”**


![](/assets/gitbook/image-86.png)


For both these yellow warnings, the solution is to ensure that there is an assessment activity in the Assessment & Feedback section of the course page. You can navigate back to your main course page and move an assignment activity using the ‘Move’ option in the activity settings:


![](/assets/gitbook/image-87.png)


There may be situations where this is not possible. For example, where the summative submission is composed of multiple shorter submissions related to a number of different topics or weeks content, it may be more logical to place the shorter assessments with the topic or week they are associated with. In such situations, you can use the course settings for the Minimum Standards and select the option ‘Moodle (other)’:


![](/assets/gitbook/image-88.png)


This will prevent the results of this report from impacting reports on your alignment with the Moodle Minimum Standards, and this detailed report will display the message:

> **“Your course settings state that students are not submitting via Moodle”**


![](/assets/gitbook/image-89.png)


If an assessment is found in the ‘Assessment & Feedback’ section, a green tick will appear in the column ‘Assessment & Feedback Section’:


![](/assets/gitbook/image-90.png)


If there are no completion criteria added to any of the assessment activities, then you will see a warning message saying:

> “You have submission links on your course page, but none have completion criteria attached. Please add completion criteria to your summative assessment by clicking the link below”


![](/assets/gitbook/image-91.png)


Against each assessment activity you will see a grey button with the word ‘none’ in the ‘Completion criteria’ column. To add completion criteria, click on the ‘None’ button, and a page will open directly at the ‘Completion conditions’ section of the activity settings. From here you can choose to add ‘manual’ conditions (the student has to manually tick the activity to mark it complete), or you can 'add requirements, through which the activity will automatically be marked as complete upon certain conditions (i.e. a submission is made, or a date is reached):


![](/assets/gitbook/image-92.png)


Once completion criteria have been added to the activity, the button in the column will change to a green button indicating whether the completion conditions are ‘manual’ or ‘automatic’:


![](/assets/gitbook/image-93.png)


Adding an ‘Assessment tag’ is not required for meeting the minimum standard unless your summative assessment is an activity other than a Moodle Assignment, a Quiz or a Turnitin submission. For example, you may be using a Checklist as a required (and therefore summative) form of assessment. By default this report will not recognise a checklist as an assessment activity. You can add this to your Minimum Standards report by adding a ‘summative’ tag, marking it clearly as a summative assessment activity. To do this, click on the grey button in the report that says ‘no tag’. This will open a new page that takes you directly to the tag settings for the activity. Here you will find a drop-down menu allowing you to add either a ‘summative’ or a ‘formative’ tag to your activity:


![](/assets/gitbook/image-94.png)


With a summative tag added, the report will see the activity as an assessment activity, and it will appear in the report just as any other assessment activity would:


![](/assets/gitbook/image-95.png)
